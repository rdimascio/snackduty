A first glove is a big deal for a young player. It is also one of the easiest things to get wrong. Many parents buy a glove that is too big, too stiff, or made for an adult. The player then fights the glove all season, and catching feels hard when it should feel fun.

Here is how I would pick a good first glove.

## Smaller is better

A very common mistake is a glove that is too big. It seems smart to buy a size up so the player can grow into it. In practice, a big glove is heavy and floppy, and a small hand cannot close it. The ball pops right out.

For most young players, a glove between 9 and 11 inches is a good place to start. Younger and smaller players should stay near the low end. If you are not sure, pick the smaller size.

## Check the fit in the store

If you can, bring your player to the store and try a few gloves on. Here is what to look for:

- The hand slides in without force, and the fingers reach near the ends of the finger slots.
- The glove does not slide off when your player lets the arm hang down.
- Your player can open and close the glove with one hand, with no help.
- The glove feels light enough to hold up in ready position for a full play.

That third point matters most. If your player cannot squeeze the glove shut, the glove is too stiff or too big.

## Soft beats fancy

Expensive gloves are often made of stiff leather that takes a long time to break in. That leather is great for an older player with a strong hand. For a young player, a softer glove that closes easily right away is a better choice. You do not need to spend a lot for a first glove.

## Throwing hand

A glove goes on the hand that does not throw. A player who throws with the right hand wears the glove on the left hand. A player who throws with the left hand needs a glove made for the right hand. Watch your player throw a few times before you shop, and check the label on the glove.

## Breaking it in

Even a soft glove gets better with use. The best way to break in a glove is to play catch with it. A few short sessions each week does more than any trick. You can also have your player open and close the glove while watching TV, or put a ball in the pocket and wrap the glove with a soft band overnight.

Please skip the oven, the microwave, and soaking the glove in water. Those can dry out or damage the leather.

## Help your player own it

Let your player help pick the glove and write a name or initials inside it. A player who loves the glove will want to use it. And a glove that gets used every week is a glove that gets broken in.

## One last thing

If the glove still feels hard to close after a few weeks of catch, it may be the wrong glove. That is okay. It is better to switch than to let your player learn to catch with a glove that works against them.
