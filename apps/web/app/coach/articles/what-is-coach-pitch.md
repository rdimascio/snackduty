Coach pitch is often the first time a young player swings at a moving ball in a real game. It sits between tee ball and kid pitch. It can feel like a big step, for players and for parents. Here is what it is, and how you can help your player get ready.

## What coach pitch is

In coach pitch, a coach from the player's own team pitches to the batter. The coach is not trying to get anyone out. The coach is trying to throw a pitch the batter can hit. The pitches are slow and easy, and they come from a short distance.

Rules change from league to league. Some leagues bring out a tee after a certain number of swings. Some do not count outs, and every player bats each inning. Some stop an inning after a set number of runs. Ask your league for its rules so you know what to expect on game day.

## Why it matters

Coach pitch teaches young players to track a moving ball and time a swing. It also teaches the basics of the game: where to run, where to stand, and what to do when the ball comes to you. Most of all, it is a chance for young players to fall in love with baseball.

There will be lots of misses. That is normal. Every good hitter missed a lot of pitches on the way.

## How to help at home

You do not need a field or special gear. A few short sessions each week help a lot.

1. **Soft toss.** Kneel to the side of your player and toss a soft ball into the hitting zone. Keep it gentle and slow. Ten good swings is plenty.
2. **Easy front toss.** Stand a short distance in front of your player and toss underhand. This looks a lot like a game pitch. Use a soft ball, and stand behind a small screen if you have one.
3. **Watch the ball.** Before each toss, ask your player to tell you the color of the ball, or a number you write on it with a marker. This helps the eyes stay on the ball.
4. **Run to first.** After a swing, have your player drop the bat and run hard through a pretend first base. Many young players stop on the base or run the wrong way. A little practice fixes that fast.

## Get ready for the field too

Coach pitch is not only about hitting. Players field, throw, and play catch. Spend a few minutes on easy catch with a soft ball. Practice ready position: feet wide, knees bent, glove out in front, eyes on the batter.

## What to say after the game

After a game, keep it simple and kind. "I loved watching you play" goes a long way. If your player struck out, talk about one good thing, like a strong swing or a hustle play. Players learn best when baseball feels safe and fun.

## Questions to ask your coach

If you are new to the league, it is fine to ask your coach:

- How long are practices and games?
- What gear does my player need?
- Is there anything we can work on at home?

Most coaches are glad to help a parent who asks.
