Young players get better at throwing and catching the same way they get better at anything: lots of easy, happy reps. You do not need a field, a coach, or a lot of time. Ten to fifteen minutes in the backyard, the driveway, or a park is plenty.

Here are three drills I like for young players. Use a soft ball to start. A soft ball takes away the fear of getting hit, and that lets your player focus on the skill.

## Drill 1: Knee throws

This drill teaches your player to throw with the upper body, without worrying about the feet.

1. Have your player kneel on the knee of the throwing side, with the other foot flat on the ground and pointing at you.
2. Stand or kneel a short distance away.
3. Your player turns the front shoulder toward you, brings the ball back with the hand on top, and throws to your chest.
4. Catch it, praise one thing, and toss it back.

Look for a high elbow and a finish where the throwing hand ends up across the body. Start close. Move back a step only when most throws reach you.

## Drill 2: Two-hand catch

Many young players try to catch with only the glove, and the ball pops out. This drill builds the habit of using two hands.

1. Stand a short distance away and toss the ball softly, chest high.
2. Your player holds the glove out in front with the throwing hand next to it.
3. When the ball lands in the glove, the throwing hand covers it right away.
4. Count how many catches in a row your player can make with two hands.

Keep the tosses easy. When your player gets ten in a row, try tosses a little to the left or right, then a little higher or lower.

## Drill 3: Step and throw

This drill puts the whole throw together, feet and all.

1. Your player stands sideways, with the glove side pointing at you.
2. Your player steps toward you with the front foot.
3. As the foot lands, your player throws to your chest and follows through.
4. After the throw, your player should end up facing you, ready to catch.

A good cue is "point, step, throw." Point the glove at the target, step toward it, then throw. Say it out loud together. Young players love a cue they can say.

## Keep it fun

A few tips that help every session go well:

- Stop before your player gets tired or bored. A short, happy session beats a long one.
- Praise effort and one small skill, not just good results.
- Make games out of it. Count catches in a row, or see who can hit a target first.
- If your player gets frustrated, move closer and make it easier. Confidence first.
- Let your player throw to you too. Kids love being the one who decides.

## A note on safety

Use a soft ball until your player can catch with two hands most of the time. Play in an open space, away from windows and cars. Make sure your player is looking at you before every throw.
